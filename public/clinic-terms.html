Eye Drop Tracker

Clinic terms

Last updated 8 November 2026

These terms apply to clinics, hospitals and clinicians who use Eye Drop Tracker in caring for patients, including the Eye Drop Tracker for Oscar browser extension. They add to the terms of use and the privacy notice. Where they differ, these terms apply to professional use. "The clinic" means the health information custodian (the clinic or clinician legally responsible for patient records), under PHIPA or the equivalent law of its province, and the people who work under it.

1. What we provide

Eye Drop Tracker is software, not a service that holds data. It runs in the clinician's browser and on the patient's phone. It turns an eye drop plan the clinician chooses into a printed calendar, a prescription, a phone plan with reminders and a chart note. The Oscar extension reads drops from the open chart in the clinic's Oscar and can add the finished plan back to that chart as an unsigned note. A full description of how it works and what it stores is on the security page.

2. Roles under privacy law

  • The clinic is the custodian of all patient information it enters, prints, saves to its chart or gives to the patient. It decides what is collected and remains accountable for it.
  • We provide electronic goods to the clinic under PHIPA section 10(4). We are not the clinic's agent, and we are not a health information network provider, because we do not enable clinics to share information with each other.
  • The software is designed so that patient information is not sent to us, and we keep no copy. We will not use or disclose any patient information.
  • The clinic owns all its records and everything the software produces for it.

3. Our commitments

  • No use or disclosure. We will not use or disclose patient information, and we will not change the software to send any to us.
  • Confidentiality. Everyone with the ability to publish a release signs a written confidentiality undertaking and protects their accounts with multi-factor authentication.
  • Notice of problems. If we learn of a flaw or event that could let anyone access, use or disclose patient information without authority, we will tell clinics at the first reasonable opportunity. We tell every clinic on our clinic contact list, post a notice on our website and the Chrome Web Store listing, and show a notice in the app. Ask to join the list when you start.
  • Public description. We publish a plain-language description of the software, its safeguards and the information it handles, on the security page.
  • Assessment. We supply our completed privacy impact assessment and threat risk assessment, which the clinic can adopt or adapt.
  • Review. In place of on-site audits, we give clinics the source code, the test results for each release and the assessment, on request.
  • Changes. We give at least 14 days' notice on the website before a change to how patient information is handled, except for urgent security fixes, which we describe as soon as they are released.
  • Ending the extension. We give at least 90 days' notice before we stop offering the Oscar extension.

4. What the clinic is responsible for

  • Checking every output. Check every calendar, phone plan, prescription and chart note against the chart before it reaches the patient or is signed. The calendar and prescription stay locked until the clinician confirms each drop read from a chart.
  • Signing the chart note. The plan is added to the note open in the encounter, or as an unsigned note when none is open. Either way it is not signed until the clinician reads it, corrects it if needed and signs it as for any other note.
  • Consent. Using the software within the circle of care relies on the clinic's usual authority to use patient information for providing care, such as implied consent under PHIPA section 20(2). The clinic decides whether a patient's express consent is needed, for example before sending the plan link by email or text.
  • Computers and accounts. Use clinic-managed computers with disk encryption and current browser updates. Use each clinician's own EMR login, with multi-factor authentication where the EMR offers it. Do not share logins.
  • Shared computers. Press New patient or close the tab after each patient. Clinician pages clear themselves after 30 minutes without use.
  • Paper. Treat printed calendars and prescriptions as records with patient information. Hand them to the patient or shred them.
  • Prescriptions. Prescriptions must meet the rules of the prescriber's regulatory college and province.
  • Privacy breaches. The clinic meets its own duties to notify patients and its commissioner, such as under PHIPA section 12. We will help with any facts we have.
  • Using it lawfully. The clinic uses the software only for the care of its own patients, in line with these terms and the law.
  • Installing the extension. Install it only from the official Chrome Web Store listing or the clinic's IT department, and only on computers used for the clinic's Oscar.

5. How the Oscar extension uses its permissions

  • It reads a chart only when a clinician opens it, with the toolbar button, the right-click menu, the keyboard shortcut or, where the clinic allows it, the Eye drops button on the encounter.
  • The Eye drops button is optional. It can be allowed only on the clinic's own Oscar address, which is fixed in the clinic's copy of the extension. Allowing it gives the extension standing access to that one address until it is removed in Chrome's settings. The clinic accepts this when it allows the button, and can use the extension without it.
  • It reads only the open patient's notes, the note being written and the medication list, through Oscar's own interface and the clinician's own login. It sees only what that clinician can see.
  • It passes the drops, the sentence each was read from, and the name and date of birth to Eye Drop Tracker in the same browser, then deletes them. It keeps the Oscar tab and patient number until either tab closes, or for two hours at most.
  • It writes to the chart only after a click, only to the patient the plan was read from, only while the same clinician is signed in, only once per plan, and only text it builds itself from a valid plan.
  • It talks only to the clinic's Oscar and to eyedroptracker.com in the same browser. It makes no other network requests.

6. Clinical safety

Eye Drop Tracker displays and prints the plan a clinician chose. It does not choose drugs, doses or tapers, and does not replace clinical judgement. Notes read from a chart can be misread, so every drop read is shown with the sentence it came from and what was assumed, and the clinician ticks each one as checked before anything is printed. A strength in the note that differs from the drug chosen is flagged. Report any error that reached, or could have reached, a patient to us at once. We keep a record of each report and what was done about it.

7. Support, changes and ending use

  • Eye Drop Tracker is free and offered without a service level. We reply to email within five business days, and to security reports within two.
  • We may change the software. Changes that affect patient information or how outputs look are described before release.
  • A clinic can stop at any time by removing the extension and no longer using the website. We hold no patient information, so nothing needs to be returned. Patient phones keep their plans until the patient removes them.
