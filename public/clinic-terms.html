Eye Drop Tracker

Clinic terms

Last updated 6 November 2026

These terms apply to clinics, hospitals and clinicians who use Eye Drop Tracker in caring for patients, including the Eye Drop Tracker for Oscar browser extension. They add to the terms of use and the privacy notice. Where they differ, these terms apply to professional use. "The clinic" means the health information custodian, under PHIPA or the equivalent law of its province, and the people who work under it.

1. What we provide

Eye Drop Tracker is software, not a service that holds data. It runs in the clinician's browser and on the patient's phone. It turns an eye drop plan the clinician chooses into a printed calendar, a prescription, a phone plan with reminders and a chart note. The Oscar extension reads drops from the open chart in the clinic's Oscar and can add the finished plan back to that chart as an unsigned note. A full description of how it works and what it stores is on the security page.

2. Roles under privacy law

  • The clinic is the custodian of all patient information it enters, prints, saves to its chart or gives to the patient. It decides what is collected and remains accountable for it.
  • We provide electronic goods to the clinic under PHIPA section 10(4). We are not the clinic's agent and do not run a network between clinics.
  • We do not receive, store, use or disclose patient information. The software is built so that we have no way to see it.

3. Our commitments

PHIPA's General Regulation (O. Reg. 329/04, section 6) sets duties for those who provide electronic services to custodians, and further duties for health information network providers. We meet all of them, whether or not each strictly applies to us:

  • We will not use or disclose any patient information, and the software sends none to us.
  • Anyone working on Eye Drop Tracker for us agrees in writing to these same limits.
  • We will tell every affected clinic at the first reasonable opportunity if we learn of a flaw or event that could have let anyone access, use or disclose patient information without authority.
  • We publish a plain-language description of the software, its safeguards and the information it handles, on the security page.
  • We provide a privacy impact assessment and threat risk assessment template, completed for the software, that the clinic can adopt or adapt. Ask us for a copy.
  • We keep these terms as the written agreement describing the software and its safeguards. A countersigned copy is available on request.
  • Each change that affects how patient information is handled is listed with its version and date before release.

4. What the clinic is responsible for

  • Checking every output. Check every calendar, phone plan, prescription and chart note against the chart before it reaches the patient or is signed. The calendar and prescription stay locked until the clinician confirms each drop read from a chart.
  • Signing the chart note. Notes added to Oscar are unsigned. The clinician reads, corrects if needed and signs them as for any other note.
  • Consent. Using the software within the circle of care relies on the clinic's usual authority to use patient information for providing care, such as implied consent under PHIPA section 20(2). The clinic decides whether a patient's express consent is needed, for example before sending the plan link by email or text.
  • Computers and accounts. Use clinic-managed computers with disk encryption and current browser updates. Use each clinician's own EMR login, with multi-factor authentication where the EMR offers it. Do not share logins.
  • Shared computers. Press New patient or close the tab after each patient. Clinician pages clear themselves after 30 minutes without use.
  • Paper. Treat printed calendars and prescriptions as records with patient information. Hand them to the patient or shred them.
  • Prescriptions. Prescriptions must meet the rules of the prescriber's regulatory college and province.
  • Privacy breaches. The clinic meets its own duties to notify patients and its commissioner, such as under PHIPA section 12. We will help with any facts we have.
  • Installing the extension. Install it only from the official Chrome Web Store listing or the clinic's IT department, and only on computers used for the clinic's Oscar.

5. How the Oscar extension uses its permissions

  • It runs on an Oscar page only when a clinician opens it there, with the toolbar button, the right-click menu or the keyboard shortcut.
  • It reads only the open patient's notes, the note being written and the medication list, through Oscar's own interface and the clinician's own login. It sees only what that clinician can see.
  • It passes the drops, name and date of birth to Eye Drop Tracker in the same browser, then deletes them. It keeps the Oscar tab and patient number until either tab closes, or for two hours at most.
  • It writes to the chart only after a click, only to the patient the plan was read from, only while the same clinician is signed in, only once per plan, and only text it builds itself from a valid plan.
  • It talks only to the clinic's Oscar and to eyedroptracker.com in the same browser. It makes no other network requests.

6. Clinical safety

Eye Drop Tracker displays and prints the plan a clinician chose. It does not choose drugs, doses or tapers, and does not replace clinical judgement. Notes read from a chart can be misread, so every drop read is shown with what was assumed, and the clinician must confirm each one. Report any error that reached, or could have reached, a patient to us at once. We keep a record of each report and what was done about it.

7. Support, changes and ending use

  • Eye Drop Tracker is free and offered without a service level. We aim to answer clinic questions within two business days.
  • We may change the software. Changes that affect patient information or how outputs look are described before release.
  • A clinic can stop at any time by removing the extension and no longer using the website. Nothing needs to be returned or deleted on our side, because we hold nothing. Patient phones keep their plans until the patient removes them.

These terms are governed by the laws of the Province of Ontario and the federal laws of Canada that apply there. A clinic outside Ontario remains bound by its own province's health privacy law, and we will meet the equivalent duties there.
