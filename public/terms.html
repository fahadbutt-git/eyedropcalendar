1. What it is for

Eye Drop Tracker helps eye care teams turn an eye drop regimen into a printed calendar, a phone plan with reminders and a printable prescription. The clinician pages are for licensed health professionals and the people working under their supervision. The patient pages and the phone app are for patients, and the people who help them, following a plan their own eye care team has set.

Intended use

Eye Drop Tracker displays and prints an eye drop regimen that a health professional has chosen, as a calendar, a phone schedule with reminders and a prescription, and shows general prescribing cautions the professional can review. It does not choose drugs, doses or tapers, analyze images or measurements, or make decisions about care.

2. Not medical advice

  • Patients should follow their eye doctor's instructions. If anything in the plan differs from what the eye doctor said, the eye doctor's instructions apply.
  • If your eye becomes very painful or red, or your vision suddenly gets worse, contact your eye clinic right away or go to the nearest emergency department.
  • Eye Drop Tracker is not licensed as a medical device by Health Canada or any other regulator.

They are drawn from Canadian product monographs, the Ontario Drug Benefit formulary and the American Academy of Ophthalmology cap colour convention, but they may be incomplete or out of date, and products differ. Confirm them against current sources and the dispensed product.

  • Prescriptions must meet the rules of your regulatory college and province, including those for signatures. Sign each prescription by hand, or draw your signature for that prescription on screen. Signatures are never saved.
  • Limited Use codes are offered for convenience. You decide whether a patient meets the criteria, and should check codes against the current Ontario Drug Benefit formulary.

4. Reminders and read aloud

Reminders are created by the patient's own phone or calendar. They depend on the phone's settings, battery, operating system and notification permissions, and may be late or not arrive. They support, and do not replace, the patient's own routine. Read aloud uses the phone's own voice, which may mispronounce drug names.
