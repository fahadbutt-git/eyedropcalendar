Eye Drop Tracker

Privacy

Last updated 3 November 2026

Eye Drop Tracker is made by Yusuf Ahmed and Fahad Butt. It is built so that no personal or health information reaches us or anyone else. This page explains what the website and the phone app do with information.

What we collect

Nothing. We have no accounts, no database and no analytics, and we do not use advertising, tracking or third-party services of any kind.

Like every website, ours is served by a hosting company (Vercel), which keeps standard request logs for a short time under its own privacy policy: the address of your device on the internet, the type of browser, the page asked for and the time. Those logs never contain a drop plan or patient details, because the plan travels in the part of the link after the "#" sign, which browsers do not send. We do not use the host's analytics or tracking tools.

We do not use cookies. The website saves what it needs to work (such as your drug list, or a patient's check marks) in your own browser's storage, which never leaves your device and is not used to track you.

For eye care teams using the website

  • Patient details typed into the website (name, date of birth, health card number and address) stay in that browser tab. They are never sent to a server. They are cleared when the tab is closed, when New patient is pressed, or after 30 minutes without use.
  • Prescriber details and any changes to the drug list are saved in that browser only, so they are there next time on the same computer. A signature drawn on a prescription is used for that prescription only and is never saved.
  • The eye care team that uses the website is responsible for the patient information it enters and prints, as it would be for any other record it creates.

Good practice for clinics:

  • On a shared computer, close the browser tab or press New patient when you finish with each patient.
  • Treat printed calendars and prescriptions like any other page with patient details: hand them to the patient or shred them, and do not leave them on a printer.
  • Record the prescription in the patient's chart as usual. "Copy for chart note" gives you the plan and its link to paste in.
  • Sign prescriptions by hand, or sign each one on screen. Signatures are never saved.

For clinics using the Oscar extension

  • The Eye Drop Tracker for Oscar extension reads a patient's notes, the note being written and the medication list from the clinic's Oscar, on the clinician's own computer, using the clinician's Oscar login. It runs on an Oscar page only when the clinician clicks its button, uses its keyboard shortcut or chooses it from the right-click menu there.
  • The drops it finds, with the patient's name and date of birth, go from the Oscar tab to the Eye Drop Tracker tab in the same browser, and are deleted from the extension once passed on. Chart note text stays in the Oscar tab.
  • Add to Oscar chart sends the chart note text from the browser to the clinic's own Oscar. Nothing is sent to us or anyone else.

For patients

  • The QR code and link on your calendar hold your drops, eyes, dose times and dates. They do not hold your name, date of birth, health card number or address.
  • The plan sits in the part of the link after the "#" sign, which phones and browsers do not send to our server.
  • Your plan, the doses you tick off and your reminders are stored on your phone only. Deleting the app, or clearing the website's data in your browser, removes them.
  • Reminders are created by your own phone. If you add alarms to your calendar, the calendar file is made on your phone and goes only where you choose to save it.
  • Reminders can show drug names on your lock screen. Turn on "Hide drug names in reminders" to keep them private.
  • Read it to me uses your phone's own voice. In a browser, it only uses voices that run on your device, so the words are not sent anywhere.

Permissions the app asks for

Notifications, so it can remind you at each dose time. On Android it may also ask to use exact alarms so reminders arrive on time. It does not use your camera, location, contacts or health records.

Consumer health data (Washington and other US states)

Some US states, such as Washington under its My Health My Data Act, ask services to explain what consumer health data they collect. Eye Drop Tracker does not collect, share or sell any consumer health data. Your drop plan and check marks are kept only on your own device, and we cannot see them.

Children

The app can be used by a parent or caregiver to manage a child's drops. It collects no information from anyone, including children.

Medical advice

Eye Drop Tracker shows the plan your eye care team set. It does not give medical advice. If you have questions about your drops, contact your eye clinic or pharmacist. See the terms of use.
