Eye Drop Tracker

Privacy

Last updated 6 November 2026

Eye Drop Tracker is made by Yusuf Ahmed and Fahad Butt in Ontario, Canada. It is built so that patient information never reaches us or anyone else. This notice explains what the website, the phone app and the Eye Drop Tracker for Oscar browser extension do with information, which laws apply, and how to reach us.

In short

  • We have no accounts, no database, no analytics, no advertising and no cookies.
  • Patient details stay in the clinician's browser, the clinic's own medical record system and the patient's own phone. They are never sent to us.
  • The plan link and QR code hold drops and dates, never a name, date of birth, health card number or address.
  • The only personal information we hold is what you choose to send us by email.

Our role under privacy law

Clinics and clinicians who use Eye Drop Tracker are health information custodians, or the equivalent under their province's law, for the patient information they enter, print and save. They decide what is collected and stay responsible for it.

We supply software that custodians run on their own computers and that patients run on their own phones. We are not a custodian, we do not act as a clinic's agent, and we do not run a network that passes health information between clinics. Under Ontario's Personal Health Information Protection Act, 2004 (PHIPA), section 10(4), and section 6 of its General Regulation (O. Reg. 329/04), people who provide electronic services to custodians must not use or disclose health information they can reach. Our software is designed so that we cannot reach it at all. We also choose to meet the duties the regulation sets for health information network providers, set out in the clinic terms, even though they do not strictly apply to us.

Laws we follow

  • Clinics in Ontario: PHIPA and O. Reg. 329/04.
  • Clinics elsewhere in Canada: the health privacy law of their province, such as Alberta's Health Information Act, Manitoba's Personal Health Information Act, or British Columbia's Personal Information Protection Act.
  • Quebec: the Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector, as amended by Law 25, and the Act respecting health and social services information.
  • Our own handling of email: the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) and its ten fair information principles, which we apply even where the law may not require it.

Where information is stored

The website and app files are served by Vercel, Inc., a United States company, so web request logs may be processed outside Canada and be subject to the laws of those places. Email sent to us is stored by Google. Patient information is stored only on devices in the clinic and on the patient's own phone, so it stays wherever those devices are.

For eye care teams using the website

  • Patient details typed into the website (name, date of birth, health card number and address) stay in that browser tab. They are never sent to a server. They are cleared when New patient is pressed or after 30 minutes without use, including when a closed tab is reopened later. Closing the tab also clears them, unless the browser is set to restore tabs, in which case the 30 minute rule still applies.
  • Prescriber details and any changes to the drug list are saved in that browser only, so they are there next time on the same computer. A signature drawn on a prescription is used for that prescription only and is never saved.
  • The clinic is responsible for the patient information it enters and prints, as it would be for any other record it creates.

For clinics using the Oscar extension

  • The Eye Drop Tracker for Oscar extension reads a patient's notes, the note being written and the medication list from the clinic's Oscar, on the clinician's own computer, using the clinician's Oscar login. It runs on an Oscar page only when the clinician clicks its button, uses its keyboard shortcut or chooses it from the right-click menu there.
  • The drops it finds, with the patient's name and date of birth, go from the Oscar tab to the Eye Drop Tracker tab in the same browser. The extension deletes them as soon as they are passed on. It keeps only the Oscar tab and patient number, so the plan can go back to the right chart, and deletes those when either tab closes or after two hours.
  • Add to Oscar chart writes the plan into the same patient's chart in the clinic's own Oscar, as an unsigned note, only after a click, and only while the same clinician is signed in. Nothing is sent to us or anyone else.

For patients

  • The QR code and link on your calendar hold your drops, eyes, dose times and dates. They do not hold your name, date of birth, health card number or address.
  • Your plan, the doses you tick off and your reminders are stored on your phone only. Deleting the app, or clearing the website's data in your browser, removes them.
  • If you send the plan link to yourself or someone else, it goes only where you send it. Anyone with the link can see the drops and dates in it, so share it only with people you trust.
  • Reminders are created by your own phone. Reminders can show drug names on your lock screen. Turn on "Hide drug names in reminders" to keep them private.
  • Read it to me uses your phone's own voice. In a browser, it only uses voices that run on your device, so the words are not sent anywhere.
  • To see, correct or remove what your eye clinic holds about you, ask your clinic. We cannot see your records.

Permissions the app asks for

Notifications, so it can remind you at each dose time. On Android it may also ask to use exact alarms so reminders arrive on time. It does not use your camera, location, contacts or health records.

Your rights

You can ask to see, correct or delete any personal information we hold about you, which is limited to email you sent us. We answer within 30 days. If we cannot do what you ask, we will say why. You can withdraw from contact with us at any time.

If something goes wrong

If we learn of a security problem that could affect personal information, we will tell affected clinics at the first reasonable opportunity, so they can meet their own duties to patients and regulators. Where PIPEDA applies to us, we will report breaches that create a real risk of significant harm to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada and to the people affected, and keep a record of every breach for at least 24 months. How we handle this is set out on the security page.

Consumer health data (Washington and other US states)

Some US states, such as Washington under its My Health My Data Act, ask services to explain what consumer health data they collect. Eye Drop Tracker does not collect, share or sell any consumer health data.

Children

The app can be used by a parent or caregiver to manage a child's drops. It collects no information from anyone, including children.

Medical advice

Eye Drop Tracker shows the plan your eye care team set. It does not give medical advice. If you have questions about your drops, contact your eye clinic or pharmacist. See the terms of use.
