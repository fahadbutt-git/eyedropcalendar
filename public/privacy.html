Eye Drop Tracker

Privacy

Last updated 6 November 2026

Eye Drop Tracker is made by Yusuf Ahmed and Fahad Butt in Ontario, Canada. It is built so that patient information never reaches us or anyone else. This notice explains what the website, the phone app and the Eye Drop Tracker for Oscar browser extension do with information, which laws apply, and how to reach us.

If you are a patient

  • Your eye clinic can see your plan. Anyone you give your calendar or plan link to can see your drops and dates.
  • We, the makers, cannot see your plan or your name.
  • Lost your calendar? Your eye clinic can print it again. More is in For patients.

In short

  • We have no accounts, no database, no analytics, no advertising and no cookies.
  • Patient details stay in the clinician's browser, the clinic's own medical record system and the patient's own phone. The software does not send them to us, and we keep no copy.
  • The plan link and QR code hold drops and dates, never a name, date of birth, health card number or address.
  • The only personal information we hold is what you choose to send us by email.

Our role under privacy law

The eye clinic is the custodian of your health information. That means the clinic is legally responsible for it. It decides what is collected and keeps it safe.

We supply software that clinics run on their own computers and patients run on their own phones. We are not a custodian, we do not act as a clinic's agent, and we are not a health information network provider, because we do not pass information between clinics.

Ontario's Personal Health Information Protection Act, 2004 (PHIPA), section 10(4), and section 6 of its General Regulation (O. Reg. 329/04) say that people who provide electronic services to custodians must not use or disclose health information. The software is designed so that patient information is not sent to us, and we keep no copy. What we promise clinics is set out in the clinic terms.

Laws we follow

  • Clinics in Ontario: PHIPA and O. Reg. 329/04.
  • Clinics elsewhere in Canada: the health privacy law of their province, such as Alberta's Health Information Act, Manitoba's Personal Health Information Act, or the general privacy law of their province, such as British Columbia's Personal Information Protection Act.
  • Quebec: Eye Drop Tracker is not yet offered in Quebec. Before it is, it will meet the Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector, as amended by Law 25, and the Act respecting health and social services information, and be available in French.
  • Our own handling of email: the ten fair information principles of the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA). We follow them even though PIPEDA may not apply to a free product.

Where information is stored

The website and app files are served by Vercel, Inc., a United States company, so web request logs may be processed outside Canada and be subject to the laws of those places. Email sent to us is stored by Google. Patient information is stored only on devices in the clinic and on the patient's own phone, so it stays wherever those devices are.

For eye care teams using the website

  • Patient details typed into the website (name, date of birth, health card number and address) stay in that browser tab. They are never sent to a server. They are cleared when New patient is pressed or after 30 minutes without use, including when a closed tab is reopened later. Closing the tab also clears them, unless the browser is set to restore tabs, in which case the 30 minute rule still applies.
  • Prescriber details and any changes to the drug list are saved in that browser only, so they are there next time on the same computer. A signature drawn on a prescription is used for that prescription only and is never saved.
  • The clinic is responsible for the patient information it enters and prints, as it would be for any other record it creates.

For clinics using the Oscar extension

  • The Eye Drop Tracker for Oscar extension reads a patient's notes, the note being written and the medication list from the clinic's Oscar, on the clinician's own computer, using the clinician's Oscar login. It reads a chart only when the clinician clicks its button, uses its keyboard shortcut or chooses it from the right-click menu.
  • A clinic can also add an Eye drops button to its encounters. This is offered only for the clinic's own Oscar address, named in the clinic's copy of the extension, and only after a clinician allows it in Chrome. Once allowed, the extension keeps access to that one Oscar address until access is removed in Chrome's settings. The button itself reads nothing until it is clicked.
  • The drops it finds, the sentence each was read from, and the patient's name and date of birth go from the Oscar tab to the Eye Drop Tracker tab in the same browser. The extension deletes them as soon as they are passed on. It keeps only the Oscar tab and patient number, so the plan can go back to the right chart, and deletes those when either tab closes or after two hours.
  • Add to Oscar chart writes the plan into the same patient's chart in the clinic's own Oscar, as an unsigned note, only after a click, and only while the same clinician is signed in. Nothing is sent to us or anyone else.

For patients

  • The QR code and link on your calendar hold your drops, eyes, dose times, dates and a random code that is not linked to your name. They do not hold your name, date of birth, health card number or address.
  • Your printed calendar shows your name and date of birth. Keep it somewhere private, or shred it when you are done.
  • Lost your calendar or phone? Your eye clinic can print the calendar again from your chart. You can also open the plan link if you saved it.
  • If you help someone with their drops, you can keep their plan link on your own phone. The same privacy rules apply.
  • Your plan, the doses you tick off and your reminders are stored on your phone only. Deleting the app, or clearing the website's data in your browser, removes them.
  • If you send the plan link to yourself or someone else, it goes only where you send it. Anyone with the link can see the drops and dates in it, so share it only with people you trust.
  • Reminders are created by your own phone. Reminders can show drug names on your lock screen. Turn on "Hide drug names in reminders" to keep them private.
  • Read it to me uses your phone's own voice. In a browser, it only uses voices that run on your device, so the words are not sent anywhere.
  • To see, correct or remove what your eye clinic holds about you, ask your clinic. We cannot see your records.

Permissions the app asks for

Notifications, so it can remind you at each dose time. On Android it may also ask to use exact alarms so reminders arrive on time. It does not use your camera, location, contacts or health records.

Your rights

You can ask to see, correct or delete any personal information we hold about you, which is limited to email you sent us. We reply to email within five business days, and to security reports within two. Privacy requests are completed within 30 days. If we cannot do what you ask, we will say why. You can stop contact with us at any time.

If something goes wrong

If we learn of a security problem that could affect personal information, we will tell clinics at the first reasonable opportunity, so they can meet their own duties to patients and regulators. We have no accounts, so we tell every clinic on our clinic contact list, post a notice on this website and the Chrome Web Store listing, and show a notice in the app. Where PIPEDA applies to us, we will report breaches that create a real risk of significant harm to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada and to the people affected. We keep a record of every breach for at least 24 months. More is on the security page.

Children

The app can be used by a parent or caregiver to manage a child's drops. The app collects no information about children or anyone else. Only email you send us reaches us.

Medical advice

Eye Drop Tracker shows the plan your eye care team set. It does not give medical advice. If you have questions about your drops, contact your eye clinic or pharmacist. Do not stop or change your drops without talking to your eye care team, even when your eye feels better.

Get help right away from your eye clinic or the nearest emergency department if your eye becomes very painful or red, your vision suddenly gets worse, you see new flashes, floaters or a shadow like a curtain, or you have halos around lights with headache or nausea.
